# Draw — Design System

This file is the visual source of truth for the Draw website. Keep new pages and changes aligned with these decisions.

## Brand

Draw is a bridge from a person’s paper sketch to digital tools that can interpret, model, and test the idea. The site should feel thoughtful, precise, calm, and ambitious: editorial typography with a light studio interface.

## Color and surfaces

- Light canvas: warm off-white `#f7f7f4`.
- Ink: soft near-black `#22231f`.
- Muted text: `#777870`.
- Accent: pale paper yellow `#e7dfa1`, used sparingly for highlights and interaction feedback.
- Dark mode uses the existing CSS variables; components must inherit them instead of hard-coding light colors.
- Glass surfaces use a translucent paper tint, a fine ink border, restrained shadow, and backdrop blur. Keep enough tint for text behind a card to become unreadable.
- Prefer rounded pills for compact controls and softly rounded cards for content. Avoid boxed rules and decorative borders that interrupt the editorial page flow.

## Typography

- Sans: Manrope, with the existing system fallbacks. Use it for headlines, body copy, and interface labels where a clean reading voice is needed.
- Mono: DM Mono, with the existing system fallbacks. Use it for navigation, metadata, small status labels, and the Vision page's editorial text.
- Serif italics are reserved for the short editorial accent in the Product headline. Do not apply italics or bold to whole paragraphs.
- Definition card: a restrained medium-weight sans title; small mono part-of-speech label; readable, regular-weight sans definition; muted secondary line. Italicize only the part-of-speech word.
- Do not add a new font family without updating this system and getting an explicit design reason.

## Header and motion

- Keep `Draw` centered in the header as the page scrolls. It begins as the large home-page wordmark and settles into the compact header position without jumping.
- The navigation becomes a narrow, light glass surface while scrolling: subtle blur, fine border, soft shadow, and a shorter height.
- The header, centered wordmark, and definition card must remain legible and within the viewport at browser zoom and at 320 px CSS width.
- Hover feedback should be small and clear: a fine underline, slight color change, or a one-pixel lift. Avoid jitter, looping motion, and pointer-driven movement of content. The one exception is the webcam's red recording dot, which blinks only while the pointer or keyboard focus is on the webcam and stops (steady red under reduced motion) when it leaves.
- The definition opens from the Draw wordmark only. It stays open while the pointer moves from the wordmark onto the card and closes when moving to Product or another control.
- The custom page scrubber is a visible, compact glass capsule with a readable track and thumb. Keep pointer dragging and keyboard navigation; do not reduce it to a tiny line.
- Drawing mode (Home sheet): the first stroke slides the header, the compact wordmark and its Product link out of the way. They return when the pointer reaches the top edge, when the page scrolls, or through the "Mostrar menu" button (touch and keyboard).
- Home exceptions to the hover rule above: each Draw letter hops on hover (and the webcam follows the "w"), and the gallery highlights one card at a time every 7 s with the same lift as hover. Both are disabled under reduced motion.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- The Product construction illustration is rendered as colored ASCII symbols on a canvas, using the same glyph-ranking approach as the Vision portrait. On entry its symbols appear from the top down, scattered, slowly over about 4.6 s, then the artwork stays still. Do not animate or parallax it afterward. Reduced-motion users see it at once.

## Definition card

- The Draw definition appears only when hovering or keyboard-focusing the Draw mark in the shared header, on every route.
- Keep the glass card centered beneath the mark, readable at compact and large viewport sizes, with no scaling tied to the wordmark size.
- The card copy should describe the paper-to-digital idea in plain language and retain enough detail to communicate the vision. The card does not belong in the Vision page body.

## Page composition

- Home places a still archive of consequential human drawings around the centered Draw wordmark. Keep the artifacts static, make each one link to its primary or archival source, and preserve room around the wordmark. Add a small webcam SVG perched on the final “w”; fade it as the wordmark settles into the compact header. Hovering or focusing the webcam opens a matching glass card that explains its role in live capture. Place that card to the wordmark’s right on desktop and below it on narrow screens, clear of the page scrubber. Keep the webcam card separate from the Draw definition card. On narrow screens, move the archive below the first-screen mark. On load, trace each archive frame as a stroke in staggered order, then let the frame's paper, artwork, and caption fade in from a blur. Let the Draw wordmark emerge from depth on first load: it starts small, blurred, and pushed back, then settles forward to full size and sharpness over about 1.3 s. On hover or focus, an archive frame grows about 4.5% and gains a soft shadow beneath it (no growth under reduced motion). Reduced-motion users see everything at once.
- Below the home archive, provide a full-viewport, edge-to-edge drawing sheet. Keep a compact floating glass toolbar for pen, eraser, color, stroke width, and one clear-all action. The sheet must remain free of cards, inset margins, and secondary action rows. It stays edge-to-edge but must read as a distinct sheet: its own tone (near-white in light, slightly lifted in dark), a soft inner shadow along its top edge marking where it begins, and a slightly darker tool tray in the last 100 px with its own soft inner shadow marking where the drawable paper ends. No hard rules or lines. Store strokes in normalized coordinates so resize does not distort them, use coalesced pointer events, and cap canvas pixel ratio to keep memory use predictable. Offer four stroke sizes (fine, medium, thick, and a very large one). Show the area the pen or eraser will cover as a round highlighter-yellow (`--accent`) preview under the pointer, and flash it briefly at the sheet's center when the size or tool changes.
- Vision is an uninterrupted editorial page: a large centered Manrope headline (medium weight, tight tracking, matching the Product headline), readable centered mono paragraphs, and a quiet mono sign-off. A local canvas renders a Sailor Moon inspired portrait as ASCII above the headline; her eyes open on entry, blink, and wink while separate waves move through the twin tails. On entry the portrait is not drawn as a stroke: its symbols appear from the top down, scattered, over about three seconds, and only then do the eyes and hair begin to move. Taper the portrait's lower silhouette so it does not end in a rectangular crop. Type the headline and copy in one measured reading order, with the full copy immediately available to assistive technology and reduced-motion users. If the reader keeps scrolling down (about 260 px of wheel, touch, or key intent, which resets after a brief pause), stop typing and let the remaining copy emerge from a blur with a 640 ms fade, leaving typed text untouched. Reduced-motion users get the full copy immediately. Do not insert a dictionary card, eyebrow, or horizontal rules into the Vision copy.
- Papers (`/papers`, the “papers” tab beside “vision” in the header) is a stage with no cards in the page body: a large centered Manrope “papers” headline, one short mono line under it that cycles every ~4.8 s (fades through a blur), and the twelve research-paper proposals from `docs/research` orbiting on a slow ellipse as small glass cards (number + short name; the ones passing in front are larger and sharper, the ones behind smaller and faint, and the title stays above those behind). The ring keeps turning under the pointer (cards are never stopped by hover, so none slips away from a click) and eases to a stop while a card has keyboard focus or its summary is open; clicking a card opens a glass panel under the headline with the title, one-sentence summary and “fechar” (Escape also closes), and the panel sits above every card. Article pages render the source Markdown with illustrations, captions, and an interactive matrix where available. Readers can download the article as a PDF. The copy must stay honest: the collection is proposals, not validated results (“ainda sem resultados”). Reduced-motion users get a static ring and a static line. The header's `vision`/`papers` links drop to 12 px under 640 px so they clear the centered Draw mark at 320 px.
- Product uses the shared pill style for its construction status, a centered ASCII illustration that settles still after its entry, a clear headline, and concise explanatory copy.
- The Product “em construção” pill opens the Draw studio (`/app`), an interface of its own with no site header or scrubber: a paper pane and a live digital pane, an ink dock and a clear action. Entering plays a blast (about 2 s): a hole tears open from the center of the page, irregular rather than a clean circle, with 3-4 random spikes that tear ahead fast while the rest of the edge creeps after, jagged, until every direction has opened and the hole covers everything; the hole fills white, ending exactly where the flash begins so the cut is seamless, and pixelated shards fly outward from its edge (more from the spikes), drawn on a canvas, emitted per second so density is the same at any refresh rate. Then the studio arrives under a white flash that fades out over about 1.3 s while the panes settle from overexposed with a small punch-in. Reduced-motion users go straight in with no blast and no flash. Leaving (“sair”) is its own sequence, not a reversed blast: the studio washes out to white (about 1.1 s, panes overexposing), then the Product page returns from far away, shrinking from huge down to its normal size while the white fades out (about 1.5 s).
- The studio dock has a “câmera” control that opens a glass card listing the detected cameras by name (asking permission first, so names can appear). Choosing one shows its live feed behind the paper pane and in the status pill, with a red dot while on; “Desligar câmera” stops it. While a camera is on, the ink swatches and “limpar” are hidden and the paper pane is view-only; they return when the camera is turned off. Video stays local by default. When cloud processing is configured, users can explicitly enable sending camera frames from the camera picker. Errors (denied, none found) appear inline in the card. The card closes when clicking anywhere outside it, on Escape, or after choosing a camera.
- The studio top bar has its own light/dark toggle (same saved preference as the site, so the choice carries back when leaving). Switching there is an explosion in waves: the new theme bursts out of the screen's center in concentric shockwave rings that spread until they cover everything (about 1.5 s, fast start, easing out), with an overexposed flash that settles, while the old theme is pushed outward and blurred. Reduced-motion and unsupported browsers switch instantly. The first ink follows the theme's ink color so strokes stay legible in both themes.
- Studio, light theme: a deeper warm-gray backdrop with a visible dot grid, near-white paper pane and a pale-yellow digital pane, each with a firm ink border and shadow so the split reads clearly, and darker labels and controls. The dark theme already separates well and is unchanged.
- Studio structure (`/app` and below, one persistent frame with the top bar): the entry screen (`/app`) shows a small webcam character slightly left of center with a speech bubble asking “O que vamos construir hoje?”, and to its right the options “Iniciar novo projeto” (`/app/new`), “Carregar projeto” (`/app/projects`) and “Biblioteca” (`/app/library`, every drawing of every project). The most recent drawings, across all projects, orbit the screen on a slow ellipse (back ones smaller and fainter; it pauses on hover/focus and is static under reduced motion) and open the drawing when clicked. A project (`/app/p/:id`) lists its drawings as cards (thumbnail, name, strokes, date) with a “Novo” card that creates a drawing and opens the editor (`/app/p/:id/d/:id`), the paper/digital panes with camera described above. The pane focus controls are keyboard accessible, and the digital preview preserves the paper's proportions when pane sizes differ. Projects and drawings live in the browser's local storage; each drawing autosaves about 0.9 s after each stroke (and on clear, leave or tab close) into itself, with no expiry. The editor bar shows a back link to the project, the drawing name (editable) and the camera status. Deleting a project or drawing takes two clicks (“excluir”, then “confirmar?”). The “Draw studio” mark returns to the entry screen. Saves from the former sidebar are moved once into a “Salvamentos anteriores” project.
- Use whitespace for hierarchy. Keep the pages minimal and avoid adding sections or labels that were not requested.

## Interaction and responsive behavior

- Navigation links and controls need visible hover and keyboard-focus states.
- The language choices opened from the theme control stay inside the viewport on narrow screens.
- The location label is not a link. On hover or keyboard focus, “São Paulo, Brazil” lifts slightly and a hairline divider with “São José dos Campos” fades in beneath it.
- Keep the location label's response restrained on desktop. On narrow layouts, prioritize the centered wordmark and prevent overlap with the theme control.
- Theme changes must work on every route and preserve contrast.
- Test critical header/card layouts at desktop width, 640 px, and 320 px, including while the home wordmark is transitioning on scroll.
- Switching theme cuts the page diagonally: a thin hand-drawn line is written from the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner in the new theme, then opens out on both sides to reveal the whole new theme (about 1.1 s, View Transitions API). Reduced-motion and unsupported browsers switch instantly.
